**1. Verdict:** GO WITH FIXES — C1–C6 contracts are coherent and shippable; the gaps are dependency bookkeeping and three unspecified runtime rules, not design defects.

**2. Critical risks**
- **Header-flush trigger undefined.** C4(b) branches on whether headers flushed, but nothing ties the flush to the first *content-bearing* event. If headers flush on the first upstream byte (e.g., an anthropic `ping`), a later pre-commit failure is forced down the SSE-error branch instead of a clean 4xx.
- **Knob hot-reload vs in-flight streams unspecified** — mid-stream semantics flip is undefined for all three knobs.
- **Hot-path accumulation:** C5 mandates one decode at close but not *iolist* accumulation; naive `<<Bin/binary, Frag/binary>>` concat copies on every fragment at 256KiB scale.
- Chat text deferral under tool interleaving adds tail latency; 3.4 asserts ordering eunit but should budget deferral latency explicitly.
- 3.5 lacks the prerequisite enumeration that 1.9/2.3 have.

**5. Architecture/design notes**

- The four index-space map is the top correctness hotspot — collision eunit against replayed upstream ids is the right guard.
