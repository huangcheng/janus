**1. Verdict:** GO WITH FIXES — the plan is coherent and mostly faithful to the codebase, but cap-overflow semantics, `prefer_proto` lossiness determination, and the C4(b)/C2 terminator wording are unresolved and must land before 1.9.

**2. Critical risks**
- **Cap semantics.** The 4MiB total-content cap for →responses has no defined overflow behavior (error vs truncate vs stop). C5 scopes it to →responses only, yet R7's interleaved-deferral rule implies a total-content cap for chat/anthropic too — deferred interleaved text on those targets is uncapped.
- **`prefer_proto`.** "Permanent lossy-pair native-preference" needs a lossiness predicate in LB/dispatch; today only the caller passes `prefer_proto => P`. As the blocked predicate shrinks, nothing tells dispatch which pairs remain lossy.
- **C4(b) vs C2.** C4(b) says "error event + terminator"; C2 says anthropic error is ALONE (no `message_stop`) and responses `response.failed` ALONE. Wording contradicts the per-target terminals.
- **Cache tokens.** C3/1.8b carry only `cache_read_input_tokens`; `cache_creation_input_tokens` (real, billable anthropic field) is dropped with no accounting.
- **Fixture realism.** 2.1 uses shorthand names (`output_item.added`) while R8 claimed corpus-only names — risk of wiring non-corpus event strings. The capture harness has no credential/refresh ownership and cannot run in the local gate.
